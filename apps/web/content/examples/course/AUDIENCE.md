# AUDIENCE.md — Practical course for first-time technical founders

## Audience name

Technical builders trying to turn a real product idea into a paid business without drowning in startup theater, generic motivation, or investor-first advice.

## Summary

This course is for engineers, designers, automation builders, and technical operators who can make things but struggle with customer discovery, positioning, pricing, sales, and focus. They do not need another inspirational founder story. They need concrete assignments, examples, and uncomfortable feedback loops that move them toward real customers and revenue.

## Primary audiences

### 1. First-time technical founders before consistent revenue

People who have a product idea, prototype, or early users but have not yet built a repeatable path to paid adoption.

**Needs**

- practical customer discovery scripts and examples
- positioning and pricing exercises grounded in a specific audience
- guidance on shipping small, sellable versions of the product
- accountability that prioritizes evidence over busywork

**Constraints**

- discomfort with sales and direct outreach
- tendency to hide in building instead of validating
- limited budget and time outside day jobs or client work
- confusion caused by conflicting startup advice

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- watching free founder videos without applying them
- building more features before talking to customers
- copying landing pages from successful startups
- asking friends, communities, or AI tools for validation

### 2. Technical solopreneurs and indie hackers with stalled projects

Builders who have launched or nearly launched multiple things but lack a reliable path from idea to paying users.

**Needs**

- diagnosis of why previous projects stalled
- tighter niche selection and audience definition
- repeatable outreach and feedback systems

**Constraints**

- skepticism toward courses after low-quality info products
- emotional fatigue from unfinished launches
- desire to stay independent and avoid venture-default advice

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- participating in build-in-public communities
- buying templates, boilerplates, or playbooks
- switching ideas when traction is slow

## Secondary audiences

- product-minded developers inside companies exploring a side project
- non-technical founders with strong product ownership, if technical examples remain understandable
- mentors or accelerators borrowing exercises, if they do not dilute the builder-first focus

## Jobs to be done / desired outcomes

- When choosing an idea, they want to know whether a reachable audience has a painful enough problem.
- When building, they want to cut scope to the smallest version someone might pay for or seriously use.
- When talking to prospects, they want scripts and confidence to learn without pitching too early.
- When pricing, they want a practical starting point instead of avoiding the conversation.

## Pains, anxieties, and constraints

- fear of rejection during outreach
- shame around selling, charging, or looking amateur
- addiction to product polish as a substitute for market evidence
- overwhelm from VC, indie, creator, and agency advice colliding
- uncertainty about what “validation” actually means

## Motivations

- turn technical skill into independent income or a credible company
- stop wasting months on products nobody needs enough
- become more commercially capable without becoming manipulative
- experience the relief of a smaller, clearer path to first revenue

## Decision criteria

- course promises are specific and believable
- examples show messy early-stage work, not polished success stories only
- assignments lead to conversations, offers, pricing, and shipping decisions
- instructor incentives are transparent; no exaggerated income claims
- time commitment fits nights/weekends or founder constraints
- community feedback is direct but not performative or humiliating

## Language and tone

Direct, practical, and anti-theater. Use “talk to five specific prospects,” “cut this feature,” “charge for the painful part,” and “evidence beats enthusiasm.” Avoid hustle clichés, fake urgency, lifestyle-income screenshots, and “anyone can build a seven-figure SaaS” claims.

## Anti-goals and exclusions

- not for people seeking passive-income fantasies or guaranteed outcomes
- not for growth-stage startups needing executive GTM strategy
- do not shame students for being introverted, cautious, or inexperienced with sales
- do not encourage spam outreach, manipulative scarcity, or misleading revenue claims
- do not make fundraising the default success path

## Evidence

- Community observation: technical founders commonly overbuild before validating willingness to pay. Confidence: medium.
- Course-market observation: skepticism is high because many founder courses overpromise outcomes. Confidence: medium.
- Startup practice pattern: customer conversations and pricing tests often reveal demand faster than additional features. Confidence: high.

## Assumptions

- Technical builders will pay for a course if it reduces ambiguity and forces useful action.
- Direct critique and concrete assignments are more valuable than motivational content.
- A niche, non-VC framing will attract better-fit students than broad “startup school” messaging.

## Open questions

- Should the course optimize for live cohort accountability or self-paced flexibility?
- What proof is ethical and sufficient without relying on income screenshots?
- How much sales training is needed before students can run useful discovery calls?
