# AUDIENCE.md — SaaS onboarding analytics product

## Audience name

Early-stage SaaS teams trying to understand why new users do not activate.

## Summary

This audience has enough signups to see patterns but not enough research capacity to diagnose every failed onboarding path manually. They need actionable activation insight without building a full data team.

## Primary audiences

### 1. Founder-led product teams

Small teams where founders, PMs, or growth leads personally review onboarding and conversion.

**Needs**

- identify where users get stuck
- understand qualitative reasons behind drop-off
- prioritize fixes without analysis paralysis

**Constraints**

- limited analytics setup time
- noisy early data
- urgency from runway or growth targets

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- checking product analytics dashboards
- watching session recordings
- asking users manually
- guessing from support tickets

## Secondary audiences

- product managers at slightly larger SaaS companies
- customer success leads responsible for activation
- agencies improving onboarding for clients

## Jobs to be done / desired outcomes

- When activation drops, they want to know the likely cause so they can fix the right step first.
- When launching onboarding changes, they want evidence of improvement without waiting months.

## Pains, anxieties, and constraints

- fear of optimizing the wrong metric
- too many dashboards and not enough answers
- incomplete tracking setup
- pressure to show growth quickly

## Motivations

- increase trial-to-paid conversion
- reduce churn caused by bad first experience
- make confident product decisions

## Decision criteria

- fast setup
- clear recommendations, not only charts
- integrates with existing tools
- trustworthy despite small sample sizes

## Language and tone

Use direct, practical language. Avoid enterprise analytics jargon. Show before/after onboarding examples.

## Anti-goals and exclusions

- not for mature enterprises needing warehouse-native BI
- not for teams with no user flow to measure yet
- avoid claiming statistical certainty from tiny samples

## Evidence

- Founder observation: early SaaS teams repeatedly ask “why aren't users activating?” in communities and advisory calls. Confidence: medium.

## Assumptions

- Teams will accept directional recommendations if uncertainty is clear.
- Setup friction is the biggest adoption barrier.

## Open questions

- Which integrations are table stakes?
- How much qualitative evidence is needed before a recommendation feels credible?
