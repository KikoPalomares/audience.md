# AUDIENCE.md — Campaign for privacy-first family photo backup

## Audience name

Parents who want family photos backed up safely without feeding them into opaque platforms.

## Summary

This audience takes many meaningful photos but worries about losing them, sharing them too broadly, or depending entirely on big tech platforms. They need reassurance, simplicity, and trust.

## Primary audiences

### 1. Privacy-conscious parents

Parents with young children who want a safe, low-maintenance way to preserve and organize family photos.

**Needs**

- automatic backup
- private sharing with family
- clear ownership and export
- easy recovery

**Constraints**

- low time and attention
- mixed technical confidence
- emotional stakes around memories

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- iCloud or Google Photos
- WhatsApp family groups
- external drives they forget to update
- no consistent backup plan

## Secondary audiences

- grandparents receiving shared albums
- privacy-focused couples without children
- family archivists digitizing old photos

## Jobs to be done / desired outcomes

- When taking family photos, they want confidence the memories will not disappear.
- When sharing photos, they want control over who sees them.
- When leaving a platform, they want easy export.

## Pains, anxieties, and constraints

- fear of losing irreplaceable memories
- distrust of AI training or ad-driven platforms
- overwhelm from photo clutter
- family members with different devices

## Motivations

- protect memories
- reduce guilt about disorganized photos
- keep children’s images more private

## Decision criteria

- trust and transparency
- simple setup
- visible backup status
- family sharing controls
- exportability
- fair pricing

## Language and tone

Warm, calm, and trustworthy. Avoid fearmongering. Use plain privacy explanations and real family scenarios.

## Anti-goals and exclusions

- do not shame parents for using mainstream platforms
- do not imply perfect security
- not for professional photographers as the primary audience

## Evidence

- Common consumer behavior: parents rely on default cloud photo products but express privacy and loss concerns. Confidence: medium.

## Assumptions

- Privacy can be a buying trigger if setup is extremely simple.
- Exportability increases trust even if most users rarely export.

## Open questions

- Which privacy promise matters most: no ads, no training, encryption, or ownership?
- How much technical detail builds trust without overwhelming buyers?
