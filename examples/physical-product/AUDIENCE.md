# AUDIENCE.md — Ergonomic travel keyboard

## Audience name

Frequent-travel knowledge workers who type for hours away from their desk and care more about comfort, reliability, and packability than gadget novelty.

## Summary

This audience works from hotels, trains, airports, cafés, client sites, coworking spaces, and short-term rentals. They have learned that “portable” often means cramped, flimsy, or unreliable. They want a keyboard that makes serious work feel normal without taking over their bag or adding setup friction.

## Primary audiences

### 1. Frequent-travel knowledge workers

Writers, engineers, consultants, researchers, founders, and operators who travel often enough that laptop-only typing creates discomfort or slows meaningful work.

**Needs**

- compact carry size that fits a normal work bag
- comfortable key spacing, travel, and angle for long sessions
- reliable Bluetooth plus a wired fallback
- fast switching between laptop, tablet, and phone when relevant
- durable case or protection during transit

**Constraints**

- limited luggage space and weight budget
- battery anxiety before flights or long workdays
- unpredictable desks, laps, trays, and café tables
- unwillingness to troubleshoot pairing before a call or deadline
- price comparison against both normal keyboards and cheap travel keyboards

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- using the laptop keyboard despite wrist, shoulder, or posture discomfort
- packing a beloved full-size keyboard and resenting the bulk
- buying folding keyboards that feel too compromised
- trying compact mechanical boards that are too loud, heavy, or fragile for travel

### 2. Mobile-first professionals with recurring typing pain

People who split work between tablets, laptops, and temporary workspaces and need a more comfortable input device without building a full ergonomic desk setup.

**Needs**

- simple device pairing and switching
- low-profile setup that does not attract attention in public spaces
- enough comfort improvement to justify carrying another item

**Constraints**

- little patience for hobbyist keyboard terminology
- sensitivity to noise in shared spaces
- uncertainty about whether the product will actually reduce discomfort

**Current alternatives or behaviors**

- tablet keyboard covers
- compact office keyboards borrowed from home
- posture accessories that solve only part of the problem

## Secondary audiences

- digital nomads setting up temporary workstations repeatedly
- conference-heavy executives who write between meetings
- accessibility-conscious users who need a portable alternative to cramped laptop keyboards
- keyboard enthusiasts only if their preferences do not distort the mainstream travel use case

## Jobs to be done / desired outcomes

- When working away from home for several hours, they want typing to feel stable, comfortable, and familiar.
- When packing light, they want a keyboard that earns its place in the bag.
- When moving between devices, they want connection changes to be boring and predictable.
- When buying premium gear, they want confidence it will survive travel and be supported if it fails.

## Pains, anxieties, and constraints

- wrist, finger, shoulder, or neck discomfort from laptop-only work
- unreliable Bluetooth pairing at the worst possible time
- loud keys in shared spaces
- travel keyboards that wobble, flex, or feel like toys
- fear of damaging an expensive keyboard in a crowded bag
- vague ergonomic claims that sound medical without evidence

## Motivations

- protect focus and comfort during real work sessions
- reduce the penalty of travel without carrying a full desk
- buy one durable tool instead of repeatedly trying cheap accessories
- feel prepared when work happens somewhere imperfect

## Decision criteria

- typing feel demonstrated through concrete comparisons, not adjectives alone
- packed dimensions, weight, battery life, and case details are easy to find
- pairing and wired mode are reliable across common devices
- key layout compromises are explicit before purchase
- noise level fits cafés, trains, and shared offices
- warranty, repairability, replacement parts, and return policy reduce purchase risk

## Language and tone

Concrete, tactile, and unsentimental. Use dimensions, weight, photos of real bag setups, desk-depth examples, sound notes, and comparisons to laptop keyboards or common compact layouts. Avoid vague “work from anywhere” clichés, lifestyle fantasy, and unsupported medical promises.

## Anti-goals and exclusions

- not primarily for gamers seeking RGB, macro-heavy layouts, or ultra-low latency
- not for buyers who only want the cheapest possible portable keyboard
- do not overpromise pain relief, posture correction, or medical outcomes
- do not let enthusiast switch debates obscure the core travel-comfort problem

## Evidence

- Product category observation: many portable keyboards optimize size before typing comfort, creating an opening for a comfort-first travel product. Confidence: medium.
- User-review pattern in travel accessories: buyers scrutinize weight, battery life, durability, and “does it fit in my bag?” details. Confidence: medium.
- Ergonomic caution: comfort claims are subjective and medical claims require stronger evidence. Confidence: high.

## Assumptions

- Frequent travelers will pay a premium if the comfort difference is obvious and the carry penalty is small.
- A credible wired fallback materially reduces connection anxiety.
- Repairability and a protective case can differentiate the product from disposable accessories.

## Open questions

- What packed weight threshold still feels “worth carrying” for the primary audience?
- Which layout compromises are acceptable: arrow keys, function row, split angle, column stagger, or key spacing?
- How should comfort be demonstrated ethically without implying medical treatment?
