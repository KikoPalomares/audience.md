# Principles

## 1. Human-readable first

A person should understand an AudienceMD file without a tool, build step, account, schema viewer, or proprietary platform.

## 2. Markdown is the source of truth

YAML frontmatter, JSON schema, parsers, validators, and generated views are optional helpers. The `AUDIENCE.md` body must remain readable and useful as plain Markdown.

## 3. Useful beats complete

A short, concrete file is better than a long, decorative one. The standard should encourage clarity, not bureaucracy.

## 4. Evidence and assumptions are different

Audience context often begins with guesses. That is fine if they are labeled. AudienceMD should make confidence visible.

## 5. Audiences are situational

People are not only demographics. Their context, goals, constraints, timing, alternatives, and decision criteria often matter more than age, location, or job title.

## 6. Reject fake personas

AudienceMD should not require invented names, biographies, avatars, or demographic theater. Include demographic details only when they are relevant, accurate, ethical, and decision-useful.

## 7. Anti-goals matter

Good audience definition says who not to chase and which tactics, claims, or tradeoffs are unacceptable.

## 8. Agents should be constrained, not merely inspired

The file should help AI systems decide what to do and what not to do. Anti-goals and exclusions are constraints.

## 9. The format should travel

AudienceMD should work in a Git repo, a campaign folder, a content calendar, a design project, a physical product brief, a community workspace, or a service business.

## 10. The core should stay small

Extensions are welcome, but the base standard should remain easy to read, copy, and maintain.
